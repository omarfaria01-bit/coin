The full reference for the GTASP10 index, the two bots and the rewards. Every number below comes from a file in the public repo; the grey note under each section says which one. Parameters live in index/params.json and the shorter rule sentences on the site are generated from it (web/rules.js).

The dev does not run the money

Fees and payouts are handled by two automatic bots: fees are claimed and split every hour, rewards are paid every day. No transaction needs the dev's signature, and the dev never chooses who gets paid or how much. The rules are public code and every transaction is on-chain, so anyone can check that what happens matches the rules. The only manual control is an emergency stop that pauses everything for everyone; any use is announced publicly.

Public system wallet: receives 100% of creator fees, buys the basket, pays holders. The dev holds no key to it in any wallet app; the key lives only on the server.

1. Universe and discovery

The index tracks the top 10 GTA 6 memecoins on Solana, chosen every hour by fixed rules. A coin is a candidate if it is a Solana token and its name, ticker or description matches the public GTA vocabulary. The list is one big set of GTA-series words: the game and its titles (GTA, GTA 6, Grand Theft Auto, Vice City), places (Leonida, Port Gellhorn), characters (Jason Duval, Lucia, Tommy Vercetti), the publisher (Rockstar, Take-Two) and meme words. The full list is keywords.strong in index/params.json.

  • A keyword counts only when no letter touches it on either side: GTA matches GTA6 and $GTA, not AGTAB. Bare VI and bare Jason are not keywords because they match unrelated coins.
  • Ambiguous words (Jason, Vice, Trevor, Franklin) count only with GTA context: a strong word, or a second different ambiguous word, in the same name, ticker or description (D37). Everyday words (Hugh, Johnson, Core, Meme) count only next to a strong word; two of them are never enough (D41).
  • In-game app and Snapmatic meme words are strong (Snapmatic, Lifeinvader, Bleeter, Eyefind, iFruit, Weazel) (D41). The reference coin buckme / $B is found through the discovery words buckme and GTA 6 bank app; no mint is added by hand (D42).
  • Discovery is top-down (D37): the system never hunts for brand-new launches. It searches DexScreener for the vocabulary terms on Solana, takes the coins with the highest 24h volume first, then the biggest market caps. A seed list and last hour's members are measured too.
  • DexScreener search returns at most 30 pairs, so a search term crowded by copycats can hide a real coin. That is why DCTY is a search term and DacityCoin is on the seed list (D44). A seed is not an allow-list: the coin still has to pass every filter.
  • No manual allow-list. index/overrides.json holds only deny entries for scams, each with the mint, the reason and the issue where kiwi approved it. The index coin's own mint is never a candidate.

Sources: INDEX_RULES.md §1; DECISIONS D35, D37, D41, D42, D44; index/params.json.

2. The filters

A candidate must pass every filter. Numbers are averages over the 24 hours before the rebalance (or the coin's whole life if younger), sampled every 5 minutes; holder data is sampled every hour. A coin that fails is listed in the public output with the first filter it failed. A missing metric counts as a failure (insufficient_data), never as a pass.

FilterRuleWhy
F1 Ageat least 4 hours since the first pool or curveNo instant snipes; some history
F2 Liquidityaverage at least $50,000, summed across pools of at least $10,000 (smaller pools are ignored)A coin you can actually trade
F3 Top 10 holdersmedian at most 30% of supply. Vaults, burn address, listed hubs and the index treasury are excluded from the count. No minimum.Limits whale concentration
F4 Trading feesat least 8 SOL in 24h, estimated as DexScreener 24h volume over all pools × 0.25%, at the DexScreener SOL priceReal activity; lifetime fees were unprovable for big coins (D38)
F5 Launch bundleat most 30% of supply bought in the creation block by more than one walletBlocks coordinated launches
F8 Authoritiesmint and freeze authority both revoked; token is a legacy SPL Token, or Token-2022 with only allow-listed extensions (F8c, D32)Nobody can print or freeze the coin
F9 Real tradersat least 50 distinct wallets swapping in the window, any size (a lower bound for big coins)Needs a crowd, not one wallet
F10 Volumewindow volume above 0 (raw Helius volume; no round-trip cleanup)Dead coins out (D35)
F11 Market cap vs liquidityaverage market cap at most 100× average liquidityA thin pool cannot carry a huge valuation (D29)

F6 (dev filter) was removed in D35 and F7 (serial-creator filter) in D38. F3, F4 and F9 were recalibrated on reference coins on 18 Nov (D38). Coverage has no minimum: whatever samples exist are used, and a coin with zero samples is insufficient_data. A current member whose new hour is insufficient_data keeps its last passing metrics for that hour; a run that errors does not replace the published file.

Sources: INDEX_RULES.md §2, §3; DECISIONS D29, D32, D35, D38; web/rules.js (filters).

3. Score, rank, entry and exit

Only coins that pass every filter are scored:

log S = 0.4·ln(MC) + 0.3·ln(Liquidity) + 0.2·ln(Fees) + 0.1·ln(Traders)

MC and Liquidity are window averages in USD; Traders is the F9 count. log S is rounded to 12 decimals, ties go to the lower mint address (byte order), and rank 1 is the highest S. Changing units (USD, SOL, lamports) does not change the ranking.

Entry and exit (buffer rule)

The index has 10 slots. A newcomer must reach rank 8 or better to enter. A current member stays while it is still ranked 12 or better and is replaced only when it falls past 12th or is beaten for its place. The first rebalance simply takes the top 10. A coin that fails a filter is never a member, so the index can hold fewer than 10 coins, or none.

On the hourly cadence a rank exit waits one more hour; a filter failure removes the coin immediately.

Emergency removal

Between rebalances a member is removed at once if its pool liquidity falls by 50% or more within 1 hour (two samples 15 minutes apart, D34), if its mint or freeze authority is switched back on, or if the data source flags it as a rug. Weights are recomputed among the remaining members; the empty slot is filled at the next rebalance.

Sources: INDEX_RULES.md §4, §5; DECISIONS D34, D35; index/params.json (membership).

4. Weights, with a worked example

Weights are a separate step from rank (D40). Each member gets a share proportional to the square root of its market cap, with an 18% cap and a 5% floor, recomputed every hour:

w_i = clip(λ · √MC_i, 5%, 18%)

λ is found by bisection so the weights add up to exactly 100%. They are published as integer basis points (bp) that sum to 10,000: each is rounded down, then the leftover points go one by one to the largest remainders, ties to the better rank. If there are fewer than 6 members (6 × 18% is under 100%) or more than 20 (20 × 5% is over 100%) the bounds cannot hold, and every member weighs 1/n. A member with an unknown market cap weighs like the smallest coin.

Worked example (illustrative market caps, not live data)

Ten members with these market caps: $100M, $64M, $36M, $25M, $16M, $9M, $4M, $2.25M, $1M, $0.64M. Their square roots are 10,000, 8,000, 6,000, 5,000, 4,000, 3,000, 2,000, 1,500, 1,000, 800. Solving for λ gives about 0.2444 bp per unit of √MC:

5. How the fee bot works

A bot on the server (D45) handles the coin's creator fees with no per-transaction approval. Every hour, at minute 20 UTC:

  1. Claim. The creator fees move into the public system wallet (pump.fun fee sharing pointed at this wallet, or the wallet is the creator itself).
  2. Split 67 / 28 / 5. 28% is kept as the SOL prize pot (the draw is off, so it only accumulates); 5% is held for buyback & burn of GTASP10 until the burn starts (D49); 67% becomes the basket budget.
  3. Buy the basket. The 67% buys the current index members through the Jupiter aggregator, split by each member's published weight_bps. The bought coins stay in the system wallet until the daily payout.

Limits built into the bot

  • Caps per swap, per run and per day; slippage at most 3%; a price-impact check.
  • It buys only from a fresh, non-dry-run index whose weights sum to 10,000, and never its own coin.
  • A slice smaller than 0.01 SOL is not bought; it waits for the next hour. A slice that cannot be bought within the slippage cap is paid in SOL.
  • The wallet must hold the pot, the basket and a reserve for fees and token-account rent, or the bot stops.
  • One run at a time. A dry mode does everything except sign and send.
  • Emergency stop. A switch pauses the bot for everyone. It is the only manual control, and any use is announced publicly.

Public system wallet: 3annmAegHQt6ru4Qd9w9fyK1FDseY7u4iyXfeeR3hJkd. It receives 100% of the creator fees. Its key was generated on the server and lives only there (file mode 600, never printed or committed). The dev holds no key to it in any wallet app. Every claim, buy and payout is a public transaction on Solscan.

Sources: ops/fee-bot/README.md; DECISIONS D45; INDEX_RULES.md §7 (67/28/5, 0.01 SOL, 3%).

6. How payouts work

Holder rewards are paid automatically, once a day, in the basket coins the fee bot bought (D46). Nobody signs, picks or changes a payment by hand: the dev never chooses who gets paid or how much.

The daily flow (all times UTC)

  • Hourly at :40 a snapshot records every holder of the coin, minus excluded wallets. Selling to 0 restarts the holding clock. For each hour between two snapshots you earn on the lower of the two balances (D47 balance-hours), so you earn from your first full hour and buying just before a snapshot earns nothing.
  • Daily at 00:10 the payout job sends what the last plan still owes, exports the finished day, makes one plan for it (carry-over from the previous plan included), and sends the transfers.
  • A missed snapshot means that hour earns nothing for anyone. A day with fewer than 2 snapshots gets no plan; its budget waits and is split the next day.

Your share

Your share of the day's budget is proportional to the sum, over the day's hours, of balance × loyalty multiplier. Holding longer multiplies the weight of your balance:

Dust, carry and exclusions

  • Dust floor. A position worth less than a token account's rent (1,513,840 lamports, 0.00151384 SOL) is not paid and no token account is opened for it. The amount carries to the next day.
  • Excluded wallets follow a published rule: team wallets, the system wallet, program-owned pool and curve accounts, and burn or exchange addresses. The exclusions are published in each payout plan. Changing the rule needs kiwi and a new decision entry.

Never twice, never more than held

A plan is made once per day, and a new one is refused while the previous plan still has unsent transfers. The sender never sends the same memo twice (checked in its ledger and on-chain), never sends more of a token than the wallet holds, keeps the fee bot's SOL, and has a per-day cap.

Public ledger and the emergency stop

Every payout is a transaction with a memo, and the list is published as "Latest payouts" on the Rewards page, with signatures. The only manual control is an emergency stop that pauses payouts for everyone at once; it cannot target a person or change an amount, and any use is announced publicly.

Sources: ops/payout-bot/README.md; DECISIONS D43, D46; engine/loyalty.py; web/rules.js (hold, rewards).

7. Verify it yourself

You do not have to trust the team. The code is public at github.com/omarfaria01-bit/coin.

  • Every hour is published. The hourly job commits index/out/<YYYY-MM-DDTHH>.json to the index-data branch. Each file has the members with rank, log_s, weight_bps and every metric with its source and fetch time, the excluded coins with the first failed filter, the changes, and any emergency removals.
  • Hashes. params_hash pins the rules, code_commit pins the code, and inputs_hash is the hash of the candidates, the previous members and the overrides. Same inputs and same params give the same file.
  • Recompute. The selection is plain deterministic code (tools/run_index.py, index/select.py, index/filters.py): check out code_commit, run it on the same inputs, and compare the output and the hashes.
  • The money. Fee-bot and payout-bot code is in ops/. Check any transaction of the system wallet against the rules above and the ledger on Rewards.
  • Known limits. Every number comes from third-party data, so if a source is wrong the index is wrong until the next run. Someone could pay to qualify by buying real liquidity, fees and traders; the 24h averages make a last-minute pump useless, but it is not impossible.

Sources: docs/design/INDEX_OUTPUT_SCHEMA.md §1–§2; INDEX_RULES.md §8, §9.

Who can change the rules

Fees and rewards follow a public rules file. Any change is visible on GitHub and only takes effect 72 hours later, so holders always see it coming. The dev cannot change payouts on the spot.

The file is econ/params.json. The bots publish the sha256 of the rules they run on, so you can check it against GitHub.

The rules hash appears here once the fee bot runs.

The bots run on our server; full on-chain enforcement (multisig with time lock) is planned.

Sources: econ/README.md; DECISIONS D50.

8. Team and disclosure

  • 0% of fees go to the team. 100% go back to the community: 67% as rewards in the basket coins, 28% to the prize pot, 5% to buyback & burn (D49).
  • The team holds about 2% of the supply.
  • Team wallets are excluded from rewards, and the exclusion list is published with every payout plan. The two team wallets are creator E8Jg…4uXJ and dev share GscV…JEks (also listed on Rewards).
  • The creator fees are paid to the public system wallet above, not to a personal wallet (D45).

Sources: web/rules.js (fee_line, team_line); DECISIONS D43, D45, D46.

9. The draw (coming soon)

Status: COMING SOON. The draw is not live and nothing is promised. It stays off until a legal review is done (legal #8). The 28% share only builds a SOL pot meanwhile.

  • One SOL prize, one winner. A pot below 0.1 SOL rolls over to the next draw.
  • Odds follow the same holding score as rewards, linear and exact. Holdings are counted up to 5% of supply, and no holder group can take more than 5% of the odds (D39).
  • A wallet needs at least 0.01% of supply to enter. With fewer than 100 eligible holder groups there is no draw and the pot rolls over.
  • Every draw is committed to a hash of all its inputs before the random seed is requested, so the result can be verified afterwards (D39).

Sources: INDEX_RULES.md §7; DECISIONS D39, D43; web/rules.js (draw).

10. Risks

  • You can lose everything. Memecoins can go to zero, and GTASP10 holds a basket of them. No return is promised.
  • The filters can be wrong. Every number comes from third-party data. A bad data point can put a bad coin in the index or drop a good one, and membership is not an endorsement.
  • Smart-contract and launchpad risk. Solana programs, pools, bonding curves and swap routes can fail or be exploited, and rewards depend on the swaps succeeding.
  • Automation can fail. A bot can pause, a day can be skipped, and the emergency stop can pause everything for everyone. Unpaid amounts are carried, not lost.
  • No advice. Nothing here is financial, legal or investment advice. GTASP10 is not affiliated with Rockstar Games or Take-Two Interactive.
